### Trace and timing diagnostics

Every hover trace contains 1,203 draws and 1,201 presents. Each of the 80 pan/zoom traces contains 1,200 draws and 1,198 presents. In every trace, draws at one-based ordinals 1 and 3 are replaced before a present. The hover traces therefore have three more events of each kind than the pan/zoom traces. T1.6's `attribute.py` expects exactly `warmup + frames` draws (1,200 here) and classifies all 20 hover traces as unusable before applying the timing rules. T1.6 owns the follow-up to align its checker with the frame boundaries the bench measures in `overlay-every-frame` sampling and keep the existing attribution criteria.

The bench still reports nonzero hover missed-refresh counts. The 100% runs 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7 report 4, 3, 28, 2 and 1, respectively. The 125% run 2 reports 1. A checker that understands the trace boundaries must still judge those intervals.

The frozen summary counts 40 late pan/zoom frames in 25 of 80 runs. Rule (b) counts 37 and rule (c) counts three. The 100% block has 15 counted frames in repetitions 2–5, which contain the reported overlap, and 16 in repetitions 6–10. The 125% block has nine after the reported W2.2 work ended. Most counted frames have isolated long draws. The affected 1k draws are generally about 9–20 ms against typical 2–3 ms, and many affected 10k draws are about 23–43 ms. A smaller set spends roughly 12–43 ms between draw end and present end. The traces resemble the T2.7 paint-stall evidence. They support a targeted traced diagnostic before any new certification attempt.
