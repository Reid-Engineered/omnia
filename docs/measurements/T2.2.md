# T2.2 spatial index: measurements

**Not the reference machine.** Q4 names the Orion laptop (plan section 2.1) as the only
machine that can mark a budget met. T2.2 has no budget of its own; these numbers record what a
viewport query costs, for T2.5 (culling) and T4.2 (hit testing, B7) to build on.

## The sheet

T2.4 owns the stress-sheet generator and has not landed, so the bench
(`crates/omnia-doc/benches/index.rs`) builds an equivalent synthetic sheet with a fixed seed:
exactly **10,000 parts and 20,000 wires** (the `10k` counts in plan section 2), no labels.

- Parts on a 100 x 100 lattice, 7 grid steps (17.78 mm) apart, 1.76 m square, inside K1.
- Part boxes sized like K7: 85% 4 grid steps, 10% 9 (op-amp), 5% 2.4 (ground).
- Per part, one horizontal wire to its right-hand neighbour and one vertical wire to the part
  below. 2% of the vertical wires are long buses spanning 20 to 60 rows (up to about 1 m, so up
  to about 50 K12 cells). Wire bounds include half the K5 stroke.
- Index: K12 cells (20,320,000 nm), 99 x 99 over the sheet.

## Results

`query` into a reused `Vec` with capacity for the whole sheet (no allocation, proved by
`crates/omnia-doc/tests/index_alloc.rs`). Viewports are 3840 x 2160 logical px; K3 gives
20 px per grid step at 100%. Times are criterion's [low, estimate, high] for one query.

| Case | Area (mm) | Items returned | Run 1 | Run 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4K viewport, 100%, sheet centre | 487.7 x 274.3 | 1,314 | [3.31, **3.33**, 3.34] µs | [4.67, **4.80**, 4.94] µs |
| 4K viewport, 100%, near a corner | 487.7 x 274.3 | 1,171 | [3.23, **3.44**, 3.82] µs | [4.32, **4.48**, 4.65] µs |
| 4K viewport, 15% (whole sheet visible) | 2000 x 1828.8 (clamped to K1) | 30,000 | [172, **176**, 180] µs (at 8%, whole sheet) | [256, **269**, 281] µs |

Run 1's third case used zoom 8% (the area clamped to the full K1 sheet, the same 30,000
items); run 2 uses 15%, which just fits the sheet's height. Both visit every item once.

At 100% zoom a 4K viewport query costs about 3 to 5 µs on this machine, roughly 0.03% of a
16.7 ms frame. Even the whole-sheet query is under 0.3 ms.
