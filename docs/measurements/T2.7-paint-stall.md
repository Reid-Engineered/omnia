# T2.7 paint stalls on Orion: diagnostic campaigns

**The paint stalls behind the T2.7 misses are the CPU clock dropping, not Omnia doing more work.** In 18 of the 20 stalls in the second campaign, the paint ran the same instructions in the same number of cycles as its neighbours. The core clock fell from about 3.3 GHz to about 0.8 GHz for one to three paints, so the paint took three to four times as long. Instructions per cycle stayed at about 2.2. One stall was preemption and one was low IPC. Neither the thermal throttle counters nor the temperature point to thermal or power limiting. The likely cause is the processor's own frequency choice under the `powersave` governor. The clock rises only during sustained heavy far-zoom paints, and a heavy paint that lands before it rises, or after it falls back, runs at about 0.8 GHz. This is evidence only and gates nothing. It has not been confirmed with an A/B run under the `performance` policy, which needs root on Orion.

## Conditions

Both campaigns ran on Orion (ThinkPad T470, Core i5-7300U, HD Graphics 620 on the Mesa Vulkan driver) under Arch Linux 7.2.7-arch1-1 in a Wayland session, on November 18, 2026. The panel was eDP-1, fullscreen at 1920×1080 at 60.008 Hz, at 100% scale. The machine was on AC. All four CPUs used the `intel_pstate` driver in active mode with the `powersave` governor and energy preference `balance_performance`. Scaling limits were 400 to 3,500 MHz. Transparent huge pages were set to `always`. The bench ran under `kde-inhibit` through `systemd-run`, as in the gate campaigns. No other user applications were running. Each run's journal entry holds its condition snapshots and `/proc/vmstat` counters. [Runner](T2.7-paint-stall/run.py) · [Classifier](T2.7-paint-stall/analyze.py)

The schedule was fixed before the first run. It had ten repetitions of 1k pan and 10k zoom at 100%, interleaved, with the pair reversed on even repetitions. Each run used 600 warm-up and 600 measured frames, with the diagnostic trace and the paint probe on. The probe (`OMNIA_BENCH_PAINT_PROBE`, `crates/omnia-bench/src/paint_probe.rs`) records the following around every sheet paint:

- wall time and thread CPU time;
- run-queue wait and timeslices;
- page faults and voluntary and involuntary context switches;
- the CPU the paint ran on;
- in campaign 2 only, the thread's user-space cycles, instructions and reference cycles.

A paint allocates nothing with the probe on. Omnia allocations were 0 per frame in every run.

A stall is a measured paint longer than both twice its local baseline and the baseline plus 4 ms. The local baseline is the median of the 10 paints on each side.

## Campaign 1: on the CPU, not waiting

Of 23 stalls, 22 were "slow, no faults": CPU time matched wall time, with no page faults and at most three involuntary switches. The remaining stall was preemption, with 3.2 ms in the run queue. Stalls occurred in 7 of the 10 10k zoom runs, mostly at frames 420 to 549, near and just past the far-zoom band. 1k pan had three, at frames 14, 22 and 480. Twelve stalls lined up with a present interval over 1.5 refreshes. One of them was B4-sized: 10k zoom run 5, frame 477, at 52.5 ms. `scaling_cur_freq` did not explain paint cost: paints cost the same at every reported frequency. Campaign 2 therefore read the hardware counters. [Analysis](T2.7-paint-stall/orion-2026-11-18-1/analysis.txt)

## Campaign 2: the clock drops

All stalls were in 10k zoom, at frames 132 to 563. The clock is cycles divided by reference cycles, times 2.6 GHz. The worst stalls came from about 3.3 GHz down to 0.8 GHz. These three caused B4-sized intervals:

| Run | Frame | Paint (ms) | Clock (GHz) | Neighbours (GHz) | Present interval (ms) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 10k zoom 6 | 444 | 23.79 (local 6.63) | 0.84 | 3.34 | 51.05 |
| 10k zoom 7 | 488 | 24.03 (local 6.97) | 0.84 | 3.35 | 43.80 |
| 10k zoom 1 | 494 | 25.16 (local 6.98) | 0.93 | 3.35 | 41.87 |

The 1k pan runs had no stalls in campaign 2. Every 1k pan paint ran at 0.77 GHz (10th to 90th percentile). 10k zoom paints also sat at 0.77 GHz at the median, but reached 3.35 GHz at the 90th percentile. The clock rises only under sustained heavy paints, at far zoom, and falls back between them. A stall is a heavy paint that runs before the clock has risen, or after it has fallen back. The same mechanism would explain the rarer 1k-pan B3 misses in the gate campaigns, where a light paint ran slower still, but campaign 2 caught none of them. ## Not thermal or power limiting

Around one extra 10k zoom run, the per-CPU `core_throttle_count` (18,583 and 364) and `package_throttle_count` (18,686) did not change. The package temperature rose from 46 °C to 60 °C. Both RAPL package limits are 25 W, above the i5-7300U's 15 W design power. RAPL energy is root-only, so package power was not measured. That run is not archived. Its readings are given here.

## What would settle it, and what it means

- **A/B under the `performance` policy.** Repeat campaign 2 with the governor set to `performance`, or the energy preference set to `performance`, which needs root. Then compare the stall count and the clocks. If the stalls disappear, the cause is frequency policy.
- **Protocol question for the owner.** Plan §2.1 records the governor for GUI gates but does not set it. CPU-only runs use `performance`. Whether GUI gate runs should do the same is an owner decision. Pinning the governor would remove noise from Orion that Windows laptops on balanced power plans would still see.
- **Product side.** A paint that fits even at 0.8 GHz cannot be hit by the drop. The 10k far-zoom paint takes about 7 ms at 3.3 GHz, which is about 24 ms at 0.8 GHz. T2.9's far-zoom stub rule removes about 70% of those quads.

## Evidence

Each campaign directory holds the order, the run journal, the binary fingerprints before and after, the status, all 20 JSON reports and logs (`raw-reports.tar.xz`), and the classifier output. Replaying either archive with the current `analyze.py` on Windows reproduces `analysis.txt`. Campaign 2's output also matched the classifier run on Orion.

```sh
d=docs/measurements/T2.7-paint-stall/orion-2026-11-18-2
r=$(mktemp -d); cp "$d/order.json" "$d/runs.jsonl" "$r/"; tar -xJf "$d/raw-reports.tar.xz" -C "$r"
python3 -B docs/measurements/T2.7-paint-stall/analyze.py "$r"
```
