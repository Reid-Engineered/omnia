# T2.7 paint stalls on Orion: diagnostic campaigns

**The paint stalls behind the T2.7 misses are the CPU clock dropping, not Omnia doing more work.** In 18 of the 20 stalls in the second campaign, the paint ran the same instructions in the same number of cycles as its neighbours. The core clock fell from about 3.3 GHz to about 0.8 GHz for one to three paints, so the paint took three to four times as long. Instructions per cycle stayed at about 2.2. One stall was preemption and one was low IPC. Neither the thermal throttle counters nor the temperature point to thermal or power limiting. The likely cause is the processor's own frequency choice under the `powersave` governor. The clock rises only during sustained heavy far-zoom paints, and a heavy paint that lands before it rises, or after it falls back, runs at about 0.8 GHz. This is evidence only and gates nothing. **Update 2026-11-20:** strongly supported by Campaign 3 under the `performance` governor and EPP (`0` paint stalls vs. `20` under `powersave`, `0` intervals over 2.5 refreshes, effective clock `3.32–3.35 GHz` across all 12,000 measured frames; note one non-paint B3-class `1k-pan` miss of `25.72 ms` detailed below).

## Conditions

Both campaigns ran on Orion (ThinkPad T470, Core i5-7300U, HD Graphics 620 on the Mesa Vulkan driver) under Arch Linux 7.2.7-arch1-1 in a Wayland session, on November 18, 2026. The panel was eDP-1, fullscreen at 1920×1080 at 60.008 Hz, at 100% scale. The machine was on AC. All four CPUs used the `intel_pstate` driver in active mode with the `powersave` governor and energy preference `balance_performance`. Scaling limits were 400 to 3,500 MHz. Transparent huge pages were set to `always`. The bench ran under `kde-inhibit` through `systemd-run`, as in the gate campaigns. No other user applications were running. Each run's journal entry holds its condition snapshots and `/proc/vmstat` counters. [Runner](T2.7-paint-stall/run.py) · [Classifier](T2.7-paint-stall/analyze.py)

The schedule was fixed before the first run. It had ten repetitions of 1k pan and 10k zoom at 100%, interleaved, with the pair reversed on even repetitions. Each run used 600 warm-up and 600 measured frames, with the diagnostic trace and the paint probe on. The probe (`OMNIA_BENCH_PAINT_PROBE`, `crates/omnia-bench/src/paint_probe.rs`) records the following around every sheet paint:

- wall time and thread CPU time;
- run-queue wait and timeslices;
- page faults and voluntary and involuntary context switches;
- the CPU the paint ran on;
- in campaign 2 only, the thread's user-space cycles, instructions and reference cycles.

A paint allocates nothing with the probe on. Omnia allocations were 0 per frame in every run.

A stall is a measured paint longer than both twice its local baseline and the baseline plus 4 ms. The local baseline is the median of the 10 paints on each side.

## Campaign 1: on the CPU, not waiting

Of 23 stalls, 22 were "slow, no faults": CPU time matched wall time, with no page faults and at most three involuntary switches. The remaining stall was preemption, with 3.2 ms in the run queue. Stalls occurred in 7 of the 10 10k zoom runs, mostly at frames 420 to 549, near and just past the far-zoom band. 1k pan had three, at frames 14, 22 and 480. Twelve stalls lined up with a present interval over 1.5 refreshes. One of them was B4-sized: 10k zoom run 5, frame 477, at 52.5 ms. `scaling_cur_freq` did not explain paint cost: paints cost the same at every reported frequency. Campaign 2 therefore read the hardware counters. [Analysis](T2.7-paint-stall/orion-2026-11-18-1/analysis.txt)

The 1k pan runs had no stalls in campaign 2. Every 1k pan paint ran at 0.77 GHz (10th to 90th percentile). 10k zoom paints also sat at 0.77 GHz at the median, but reached 3.35 GHz at the 90th percentile. The clock rises only under sustained heavy paints, at far zoom, and falls back between them. A stall is a heavy paint that runs before the clock has risen, or after it has fallen back. The same mechanism would explain the rarer 1k-pan B3 misses in the gate campaigns, where a light paint ran slower still, but campaign 2 caught none of them. ## Not thermal or power limiting

Around one extra 10k zoom run, the per-CPU `core_throttle_count` (18,583 and 364) and `package_throttle_count` (18,686) did not change. The package temperature rose from 46 °C to 60 °C. Both RAPL package limits are 25 W, above the i5-7300U's 15 W design power. RAPL energy is root-only, so package power was not measured. That run is not archived. Its readings are given here.

## Campaign 3: `performance` governor A/B (2026-11-20)

**Pinning the CPU governor and EPP to `performance` eliminated all 20 paint stalls observed in Campaign 2 (20 → 0) and all B4-sized (> 2.5 R) present intervals (3 → 0).** Orion ran the identical 20-run schedule and release binary (`73d610d6b0f7f2a22b8a868bdbb32b0f75a920d1adba78ebc7bf3350ee89a7e5`, checkout `91cddf86baba8ed0c135767ae7a66a6b0a0156ab`) from 22:15:42 to 22:22:48 EST on November 20, 2026, on AC, fullscreen eDP-1 at 60.008 Hz, 100% scale, under `kde-inhibit`. All four CPUs were switched to `scaling_governor=performance` and `energy_performance_preference=performance` for the campaign and restored and verified back at `powersave` / `balance_performance` immediately on exit. Caveat: Campaigns 2 and 3 ran on different days and were not interleaved, so this is a strong single A/B rather than an interleaved trial. **Non-paint B3-class miss in `1k-pan-9` (frame 581).** Campaign 3 recorded one `1k-pan` interval over 1.5 refreshes (`25.72 ms` / `1.54 R` in `1k-pan-9` frame 581), compared with `0` in Campaign 2. Under the T1.6 attribution rule this frame is **counted** (clause (b) fails because the native frame signal arrived `13.625 ms` after the previous present end). Unlike the 2026-11-18 gate's B3 blocker (`12.55 ms` draw), this miss was **not a paint stall**: `paint_probe` recorded `1.30 ms` wall / `1.31 ms` CPU, `0` faults, `0` context switches, and `3.35 GHz` effective clock; `draw` took `1.54 ms`, while `present` took `10.52 ms`. Pinning `performance` therefore removed the `powersave` paint stalls, but does not by itself guarantee immunity to occasional late-signal / long-present B3 intervals (none occurred across all 40 `1k` runs in the subsequent 2026-11-20 gate campaign).

Thermal and power telemetry around Campaign 3 (archived in [`telemetry.json`](T2.7-paint-stall/orion-2026-11-20-performance/telemetry.json)): AC remained online (`1`); `x86_pkg_temp` read `53.0 °C` before, `57.0 °C` at start, `44.0 °C` at end, and `48.0 °C` immediately after restoration; `package_throttle_count` incremented from `0` to `14` (`core_throttle_count` `0 → 14` on Core 0, `0` on Core 1), yet no paint stall or > 2.5 R interval occurred.

The remaining `22–33` two-refresh (`30–34.48 ms`) intervals per `10k-zoom` run persisted with zero paint stalls and a pinned `3.35 GHz` clock, showing they come from the normal far-zoom quad load (`8.1–10.2 ms` peak paint + GPU/compositor work exceeding one `16.66 ms` refresh), separate from the DVFS stalls.

The archive (`orion-2026-11-20-performance/raw-reports.tar.xz`) is 1,582,400 bytes with SHA-256 `b45016e3bae2c23547c9243cf34d7730ae092086a25a082b6baea69dc873c9b1`, and replays with `analyze.py` byte-identically to `analysis.txt`.

## Resolution and what it means

- **A/B result.** Campaign 3 strongly supports that the Campaign 1–2 paint stalls were caused by `powersave` CPU frequency policy (20 → 0 paint stalls, 3 → 0 intervals > 2.5 R), while also surfacing one non-paint B3-class `1k-pan` miss (`25.72 ms` from a `13.6 ms` signal gap + `10.5 ms` present).
- **Owner decision (2026-11-20).** Canvas plan §2.1 now requires the `performance` CPU governor for Orion GUI gate runs (matching CPU-only runs). The subsequent fresh 120-invocation T2.7 Orion gate under `performance` passed every budget at both 100% and 125% with zero intervals over each budget's limit (`1.5 R` for B3, `2.5 R` for B4; see [`T2.7-wire.md`](T2.7-wire.md)).
- **Product side.** T2.9's far-zoom stub rule remains planned so balanced/`powersave` laptops stay inside budget even if a far-zoom paint lands at `0.8 GHz`, and to eliminate the remaining 2-refresh `10k-zoom` drops and remove the B4 known limit.
